Editor's Note

Raul Rosas Jr. was losing on all three scorecards when the fifth round started Saturday night at the Meta Apex. A minute and 38 seconds later he had won the fight, and Raoni Barcelos was being carried out of the Octagon on a stretcher. Barcelos was taken to a Level 1 trauma center, and the UFC said later that night that he was stable, alert and responsive, with full movement. That is the best news of the weekend, and it arrived before anything else did.

It was a busy two days everywhere else. Zuffa Boxing took its 11th card to the Copper Box Arena in London, where Johnny Fisher won a majority decision over Michael Pirotton in a fight that left Fisher too spent to do a post-fight interview. In Tokyo, Takuma Inoue got up from a seventh-round knockdown to beat Tenshin Nasukawa again and keep the WBC bantamweight title. And three days after Anthony Joshua and Tyson Fury were finally announced for December 11 in Cardiff, the fight already has a promoter problem, and the promoter is Dana White.

A correction from our September 14 issue: we had the results of two Noche UFC fights backward. Alexa Grasso beat Manon Fiorot by unanimous decision (29-28 on all three cards), and Curtis Blaydes beat Waldo Cortes Acosta by unanimous decision (29-28 on all three cards). We regret the errors.

Main Story

ROSAS JR. RALLIES TO STOP BARCELOS IN THE FIFTH

Raul Rosas Jr. stopped Raoni Barcelos at 1:38 of the fifth round on Saturday night at the Meta Apex in Las Vegas, slamming him to the canvas out of a clinch against the fence in a UFC Fight Night main event he was losing on all three scorecards. Referee Herb Dean saw that Barcelos was not moving and waved it off immediately. Rosas, 21, improved to 13-1 with his sixth straight win. Barcelos fell to 22-6.

It had been one of the best fights of the year until the ending took the air out of the building. Both men traded hard through the first round, and Barcelos caught a kick early in the second and turned it into a takedown. Rosas' corner told him to stop brawling before the third, and Barcelos made him pay for listening, jumping on him with right hands and trip takedowns from the clinch. By the fourth Barcelos was taking Rosas' back repeatedly, threatening a rear-naked choke and grinding a face crank while blood ran down Rosas' face. Barcelos was fighting through a badly swollen left eye and was one round from winning.

Rosas came out for the fifth like a man who knew it. He pressed forward, tied Barcelos up against the fence and lifted him. Barcelos' head appeared to hit the canvas or the cage on the way down, and he did not move afterward. He stayed on the mat for several minutes before he was stretchered out and taken to the hospital, where the UFC said he underwent a CT scan and remained under medical supervision. His coach, Davi Ramos, posted late Saturday that Barcelos was doing well.

"I'm 21 and putting on performances like that on main events. So, hey, I'm going to need those $100,000 [bonus checks] and a shot at the top 6 or top 7."

That was Rosas afterward, after he had also asked for prayers for Barcelos. The request is fair on the merits. A 21-year-old who wins a five-round main event he was losing on every card has earned a ranked opponent. The part he cannot control is what the stoppage looked like. A comeback win by slam will be replayed for the wrong reasons for a while.

Elsewhere on the card, Ailin Perez won a unanimous decision over Norma Dumont, 29-28 on all three cards. Luis Hernandez choked out Sedriques Dumas with a standing guillotine in 57 seconds. Ilimbek Akylbek Uulu won by disqualification when Mahammadali Osmanli landed an illegal knee at 2:19 of the first round. Montel Jackson stopped Ricky Simon in the second round, and Tina Black stopped Melissa Amaya in the second.

In London on the same night, Johnny Fisher (15-1) won a majority decision over Michael Pirotton in the main event of Zuffa Boxing 11, 98-92, 96-95 and 95-95 over ten heavyweight rounds, in his first fight with the promotion. Pirotton was cut over the left eye in the fourth. Fisher struggled off his stool late, needed a seat at ringside to recover after the decision was read and skipped his post-fight interview. Radivoje Kalajdzic won a unanimous decision over Craig Richards at light heavyweight, 97-92 on all three cards, and dropped Richards with ten seconds left for good measure. In Birmingham, Liam Davies stopped Nathaniel Collins in the seventh round.

In Tokyo, Takuma Inoue kept the WBC bantamweight title with a unanimous decision over Tenshin Nasukawa in their rematch, 116-111, 116-111 and 114-113. Nasukawa dropped Inoue with a left hand early in the seventh round. Inoue survived it, led 77-74 on all three cards under the WBC's open scoring after eight, and held on through a closer finish than the early lead suggested. More on him below.

Legal Tracker

Active Cases

Hardy v. Most Valuable Promotions et al. (N.D. Tex., Dallas Division, filed Sept. 23, 2026)
New this week
Former WBO featherweight champion Heather Hardy sued Most Valuable Promotions, co-founder Jake Paul, chief operating officer Bryce Holden and Holden Boxing for more than $10 million over her August 5, 2023 rematch with Amanda Serrano on the Paul vs. Nate Diaz undercard in Dallas. The 54-page complaint alleges the fight caused Hardy, now 44, a permanent brain injury and ended her career, and that her longtime promoter Lou DiBella refused to help make the rematch because he considered it unsafe. It alleges MVP bypassed him and used Holden Boxing, the licensed promoter of record, as a liability shield. Hardy brings five counts: gross negligence, negligent undertaking, negligence per se, ordinary negligence and a violation of the Muhammad Ali Boxing Reform Act. She also asks for an injunction. CompuBox had Serrano landing 242 power punches in that fight. The fight is more than three years old, and Hardy argues the claims are timely because her lawyers did not uncover MVP's role until March 2026. MVP said in a statement that the fight was arranged through DiBella, that Hardy was medically cleared by the commission and that the bout ran under its supervision.
Status: Complaint filed

Johnson v. Zuffa, LLC, No. 2:21-cv-01189 (D. Nev.)
Evidence-destruction hearing set for October 8-9
The post-2017 fighters' antitrust case against the UFC still has no certified class and no trial date. The next date that matters is an October 8-9 hearing on the plaintiffs' motion for severe sanctions over allegedly destroyed evidence, according to a Settlement Insight review of the docket published Sunday. Judge Richard Boulware's remedy turns on whether the missing records were lost with intent to deprive the plaintiffs or through negligence. The earlier case, Le v. Zuffa, has paid $237.4 million of its $375 million settlement to 984 of 1,088 claimants, per the claims administrator's most recent update. The other 104 are held up by bank problems, legal disputes and U.S. sanctions rules.
Status: Sanctions hearing October 8-9

No suspensions or adverse findings connected to Saturday's cards had been reported by the Nevada commission, the British Boxing Board of Control or VADA as of Monday morning.

Business Intel

JOSHUA-FURY HAS A DATE, A STADIUM AND A PROMOTER NOBODY AGREED ON

Anthony Joshua and Tyson Fury were announced Thursday for Friday, December 11 at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff, a 12-round heavyweight fight that has been talked about for a decade. The press release named Dana White, working with Turki Alalshikh's The Ring, as the promoter. Within a day, reports said the fight was in jeopardy, and the reason was the name on the press release.

It has been widely reported that Joshua's contract bars White, Zuffa and TKO from promoting the fight. Joshua's promoter is Eddie Hearn of Matchroom. Fury, for his part, has said he scrapped a clause in his own contract so White could be involved. White took questions about it at the Zuffa Boxing 11 press conference in London on Saturday and did not enjoy them.

"Have I not answered this question 257,000 times? Same as it was yesterday. And the day before. And the day before that."

He said, "We're promoting the fight right now." Asked about the clause in Joshua's contract, White said, "Eddie Hearn says lots of things." He also said he did not book the stadium. Cardiff came into play after Wembley proved unworkable and Team Joshua rejected Madison Square Garden.

This is the business story underneath the fight. White and Alalshikh have spent 2026 building Zuffa Boxing as a promotion that owns its fighters and its title inventory. Joshua-Fury is the biggest fight in British boxing, and White's name on it would be the promotion's largest event yet. Hearn's leverage is a contract. Alalshikh's is the money. White's is that he is already on the poster.

There is also a belt to sell. Reports have the WBA preparing to put its 'super' heavyweight title on the fight, a title Usyk gave up in June, while Gassiev still holds the WBA's 'world' title. The WBA's own president said in December 2024 that 67 percent of the organization's gross revenue came from sanctioning fees, which are charged as a percentage of the purses. On a fight Frank Warren says will pay both men more than any British fighter has ever earned, that is a very good reason to find a belt.

Zuffa Boxing, meanwhile, keeps building in the UK without Joshua or Fury. Saturday was its third card outside the United States, and it has already announced a November 7 show in London.

Fighter Spotlight

TAKUMA INOUE

Takuma Inoue got up from a seventh-round knockdown in Tokyo and won the rest of the fight. That is the short version of his unanimous decision over Tenshin Nasukawa, 116-111, 116-111 and 114-113, and it is the best evidence yet that the WBC bantamweight champion is more than the younger brother of Naoya Inoue.

He is 30 and now 23-2 with five knockouts, which tells you most of what you need to know about his style. Takuma does not end fights. He wins rounds, with fast combinations on the inside and a right hand that kept finding Nasukawa late. He won the vacant title against Nasukawa last November and defended it in May with a wide decision over Kazuto Ioka on the undercard of his brother's undisputed fight with Junto Nakatani at the Tokyo Dome.

The rematch tested something the first fight did not. Nasukawa, 28 and a former kickboxing champion, had improved enough to beat Juan Francisco Estrada in April, and his southpaw left hand won him several of the early rounds. Early in the seventh it put Inoue down hard. Referee Michael Griffin let it continue, Inoue got to the ropes, took more shots and made it to the bell. By the end of the eighth, the WBC's open scoring had Inoue ahead 77-74 on all three cards.

Nasukawa needed all four remaining rounds and did not get them. He made the late rounds closer, enough that one judge ended up at 114-113, but Inoue's right hand kept finding him and Inoue boxed carefully through the 12th. Survival is a skill, and plenty of champions with better knockout numbers have never had to show they have it. Takuma just did.

Editor's Note

Teofimo Lopez fought at welterweight for the first time on Saturday night and left T-Mobile Arena with the WBA title and a third division on his record. He outworked Rolly Romero over twelve rounds for a majority decision, survived the one bad round Romero was always going to give him, and made himself a three-weight world champion at 29. Romero called it a robbery. The scorecards say otherwise, and so does CompuBox.

The more interesting part is who was standing on the promoter's side of the table. Lopez fought Saturday under a multi-fight deal he signed directly with DAZN in July through his own company, Takeover Promotions. No promoter took a cut. A titleholder in a marquee division just proved he can put a pay-per-view together himself, and every manager in boxing was watching to see whether it worked.

Elsewhere: Amanda Serrano got to 50 wins the hard way in Temecula, Gregory Rodrigues took a five-round decision off Anthony Hernandez in Sacramento in a fight that earned both men a bonus, and boxing's split personality on pricing is about to get its clearest demonstration yet. Romero and Lopez cost $79.99. Ryan Garcia and Conor Benn, three weeks from now, cost nothing extra at all.

Main Story

LOPEZ OUTBOXES ROMERO, TAKES WBA 147 TITLE AND A THIRD DIVISION

Teofimo Lopez won the WBA welterweight title from Rolando "Rolly" Romero by majority decision on Saturday night at T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas, taking a belt in a third weight class in his first fight at 147 pounds. Judge Glenn Feldman had it 114-114. The other two cards read 115-113 and 116-112 for Lopez.

Lopez (23-2, 13 KOs) fought the fight he needed to fight and almost nothing more. He worked behind the jab, banked rounds, and spent the second half of the night digging to the body to take the legs out from under a man who needs only one clean shot to change an evening. CompuBox had him landing 124 of 530 to Romero's 73 of 352. Romero (17-3, 13 KOs) threw 178 fewer punches and landed 51 fewer.

The fifth round was the exception, and it was a bad one. Romero landed a left hook that opened a cut over Lopez's right eye, then followed with a right hand that visibly hurt him. For about ninety seconds it looked like the fight Romero had been promising all camp. Lopez came out for the sixth, went back to the jab, and did not hand a round over again.

Romero was not interested in the punch stats afterward. "When I heard 114-114, I knew it was a robbery," he said, calling the judging "all political" and claiming he "got hit with two punches the whole fight that barely grazed me." He wants a rematch and says he is owed one. He is not. There is no rematch clause in the bout agreement, which means Lopez would have to volunteer for it, and Lopez has already said no.

What Lopez wants instead is the winner of Ryan Garcia and Conor Benn on September 12, then WBO champion Devin Haney and IBF champion Liam Paro after that. That is the undisputed route and he said the word out loud. He also said he needs time off, with a child due. Both things can be true.

Lopez has now held titles at lightweight, junior welterweight and welterweight. He was undisputed at 135 in 2020, lost the run to George Kambosos Jr. a year later, rebuilt it at 140, and has spent three years being told the version of him that beat Vasiliy Lomachenko was gone for good. Saturday was not that version. It was a disciplined, unspectacular, twelve-round professional win, which at welterweight against a puncher is worth more than a highlight.

"If you lose and you come back, you never lost, because you're a winner."

On the undercard, Gary Antonio Russell won the interim WBA junior featherweight title with a shutout of Victor Santillan, 118-109 and 119-108 twice. Yoenli Hernandez took a majority decision over Francisco Daniel Veron, and Carlos Utria edged Israel Mercado on a split. The card streamed on DAZN pay-per-view and Prime Video at $79.99.

Legal Tracker

Active Cases

Mathur v. Rechnitz (Cal. Super. Ct., filed August 2026)
No public response from the defendants
Jas Mathur, chief executive of Manny Pacquiao Promotions, is suing Jona Rechnitz, formerly Floyd Mayweather's business manager, and Rechnitz's father Robert for more than $8.3 million. The complaint alleges fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, breach of a prior settlement agreement, conversion and theft running back nearly a decade, and asks for punitive damages on top. Among the specific claims: that Rechnitz used a blank Mayweather check to take five of Mathur's watches and pawned them to cover personal debts, that he seized and liquidated a $1 million Ferrari in retaliation, and that settlement money was routed through shell entities.
The allegation that matters to the sport is that Rechnitz sold the same fight to two parties and took payment from both while also drawing advances from third-party lenders. Mayweather against Pacquiao was postponed indefinitely on June 26. This filing is the first public accounting of why. Mathur is represented by Kendall Brill & Kelly.
Status: Filed, awaiting responsive pleading

Johnson v. Zuffa, LLC (D. Nev., filed June 23, 2021)
No new docket activity reported this week
The second UFC antitrust class action, covering fighters who competed on or after July 1, 2017, remains live and remains the more dangerous of the two for the promotion, because it seeks injunctive relief alongside damages. The February sanctions motion is still the thread to watch: plaintiffs allege TKO Operating Co., Endeavor Group Holdings and Zuffa destroyed years of evidence relevant to the case. Meanwhile the $375 million Le v. Zuffa settlement has moved into distribution, with a projected average payout around $231,000 and a median closer to $86,000.
Status: Discovery / sanctions motion pending

No major suspensions or disciplinary actions were announced by the Nevada, California or New York state athletic commissions in the past week, and no new adverse findings were reported by VADA or USADA involving major combat sports athletes.

Rumor Mill

CONFIDENCE 0.85

There will be no Romero rematch. Lopez turned it down within a day of the decision, there is no rematch obligation in the contract, and his stated priority is the Garcia-Benn winner on the road to undisputed. Romero has leverage only if a promoter wants to buy the fight, and Lopez is now his own promoter, which means the man Romero has to convince is the man who just beat him. Treat the rematch talk as Romero negotiating in public.

CONFIDENCE 0.55

Devin Haney against Liam Paro is in early negotiation for October in the United States, a WBO-IBF welterweight unification. Paro has said publicly that he wants it and hears nothing back, and Haney turned to him only after Shakur Stevenson and Keyshawn Davis both closed the door. That is not the profile of a fight two camps are racing to sign. It is, however, the only unification available to either man right now, and Lopez taking the WBA belt just made the 147-pound picture urgent for everyone holding a piece of it.

CONFIDENCE 0.30

Mayweather against Pacquiao is being floated again for late September in Las Vegas, with T-Mobile Arena on the 25th and MGM Grand Garden on the 26th both mentioned and Netflix still attached. This fight has already been announced, moved off the Sphere, disputed as to whether it is a professional bout or an exhibition, postponed indefinitely in June, and is now the subject of an $8.3 million fraud suit over how it was sold. Believe a date when tickets go on sale.

Fight Card Previews

What's on Deck

Moses Itauma vs. Filip Hrgovic (August 29)

The vacant IBF heavyweight title at The O2 in London, on DAZN pay-per-view at £24.99 in Britain and $59.99 in the States. Itauma is 21 and has never been past the early rounds against anyone capable of holding him there. Hrgovic is 34, has one loss, spent years as the IBF's top-ranked contender, and stopped Dave Allen in three at Doncaster in May. Queensberry has spent two years building Itauma as the next thing in the division and has now put him behind a paywall against the first opponent with a real argument. If Itauma is what they say he is, this is the night he proves it. If he is not, the timing will look reckless in hindsight.

Mikaela Mayer vs. Chantelle Cameron (August 29)

A three-belt junior middleweight unification at bp pulse LIVE in Birmingham, headlining MVP's all-female card built as Britain against the United States. Cameron (22-1, 8 KOs) holds the WBO title and brings the pressure and the physical strength. Mayer (22-2, 5 KOs) holds the WBA and WBC and brings the jab, the volume and the better engine. Ten rounds at two minutes, which favors whoever starts fast. Cameron says Mayer is not special enough to beat her. Mayer says it will not be close. Sky Sports in the UK, ESPN+ in the States.

Umar Nurmagomedov vs. Song Yadong (August 29)

The UFC returns to Shanghai with two of the top six bantamweights. Nurmagomedov (20-1) is the more complete fighter and by a distance the better grappler, though the aura went with his loss to Merab Dvalishvili. Song has the crisper hands and the building. With Petr Yan holding the belt, the winner is realistically one fight from a title shot, and the UFC is leaning on Song's draw in China to make a Fight Night matter internationally.

Ryan Garcia vs. Conor Benn (September 12)

Garcia defends the WBC welterweight title at T-Mobile Arena on Mexican Independence Day weekend, his first time headlining that date. Benn is 25-1 with 14 knockouts, avenged his only loss to Chris Eubank Jr., and beat Regis Prograis in his Zuffa Boxing debut. This is a Zuffa Boxing promotion, so it streams on Paramount+ at no extra charge in the United States and on DAZN in the UK and Ireland. Whoever wins is next in line for Lopez, if Lopez means what he said on Saturday night.

Business Intel

LOPEZ CUT OUT THE PROMOTER AND WON A TITLE ANYWAY

The result on Saturday was a three-division championship. The precedent was something else. Lopez fought Romero under an exclusive multi-fight agreement he signed on July 20 directly with DAZN, through his own company, Takeover Promotions LLC. Reported value is about $20 million. There is no promoter in that sentence. Top Rank did not sign it, Matchroom did not sign it, PBC did not sign it. A fighter negotiated with a broadcaster and kept the piece a promoter would otherwise have taken.

Fighters have tried self-promotion before and it has usually meant a vanity company, a promotional license, and a schedule of opponents nobody wanted to watch. What is different here is the counterparty. DAZN does not need a promoter to bring it a fight. It needs inventory, and it runs a subscription business measured on how much inventory it can put in front of people. A titleholder who can deliver himself is worth more to a streamer than he is to a promoter who has to resell him.

"This is the beginning of a new era for The Takeover," Lopez said when the deal was announced. That is the sort of line fighters say. The difference is that five weeks later he had a world title in a third division and the network had a pay-per-view, and nobody in between had to be paid for making the introduction.

Watch what it does to leverage. Romero wants a rematch, has no contractual right to one, and the person who decides is the opponent rather than a promoter balancing a roster. Lopez says he wants the Garcia-Benn winner, then Haney, then Paro. Under the old structure a promoter's other obligations would have shaped that queue. Under this one the only question is whether DAZN wants to buy the fight. Cleaner for Lopez. Considerably worse for anyone hoping to be matched into a payday.

The pricing picture around all of it stayed incoherent, which is the honest state of the sport in August 2026. Romero and Lopez cost $79.99 on DAZN and Prime. Itauma and Hrgovic cost $59.99 this Saturday. Three weeks after that, Ryan Garcia defends a welterweight title on Mexican Independence Day weekend and it costs nothing extra, because Zuffa Boxing sits on Paramount+ and TKO decided a year ago to stop selling events one at a time. The UFC has already finished this argument internally. Boxing is running both models on the same calendar and letting the customer work out which one is the real price.

Fighter Spotlight

AMANDA SERRANO

Amanda Serrano won her 50th professional fight on Saturday at Pechanga Resort Casino in Temecula, and it was not the coronation the card was built to be. Lucrecia Manzur, ten years younger and 14-4 coming in, spent seven rounds landing overhand rights and lead hooks, snapped Serrano's head back more than once and buckled her knees. Manzur was ahead on the arithmetic through seven. Serrano fought the last stretch with a hand injury and took a majority decision, 116-112, 116-112 and 114-114, to keep her unified featherweight titles and reach 50-4-1.

Fifty wins is a number almost nobody reaches. "It's a milestone. A lot of people don't even make it to that," she said afterward, and she is right in a way that undersells it. She has won titles in seven weight classes. She has fought as low as 115 pounds and as high as 140. She has been the marquee name on two of the largest women's boxing events ever staged, and she is still most of the argument for why those events exist at all.

The record she came for is still out there. Serrano has 32 knockouts, tied with Christy Martin for the most in women's boxing history, and she has been tied for a while now. She is 37. She went twelve hard rounds to get win number 50. The stoppages have gotten scarcer as the opposition has gotten better and the fights have gotten bigger, which is what happens to every puncher who becomes a headliner. One more and the record is hers alone.

She talked about fighting again, maybe in Puerto Rico, then said the thing fighters say near the end: the mind wants one thing and the body does another. The fight went out free on TikTok, where MVP says 3.4 million people watched her get to 50, which is a larger audience than most of boxing's paid cards will see this year. She has been paid properly for this stretch of her career, which was never guaranteed for a woman who turned pro in 2009 and spent a decade fighting for four figures. Whatever she does next, the arithmetic finally runs in her favor.

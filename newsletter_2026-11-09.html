Main Story

UFC Revenue Jumps 60% to $519 Million as the Paramount Money Arrives

The UFC brought in $519.2 million in revenue in the third quarter, up 60% from $325.2 million a year earlier, its parent company TKO Group Holdings reported on Nov. 4. Adjusted EBITDA, the operating profit figure TKO reports, rose 72% to $285.3 million. That works out to 55 cents of profit on every dollar of revenue, up from 51 cents a year ago. TKO release

The biggest piece is television. Media rights revenue rose 66%, driven by the first year of the seven-year, $7.7 billion Paramount deal that replaced ESPN and pay-per-view in January. That deal averages about $1.1 billion a year.

Ticket money rose faster. Live events revenue was up 83%. UFC 329 at T-Mobile Arena, Conor McGregor's return, did a $26 million gate, the largest in UFC history, and TKO said every event it held outside the Meta Apex in the quarter sold out. Partnership revenue rose 29%. TKO also told investors UFC 329 drew nearly 16 million viewers to Paramount+ and peaked at 8.3 million concurrent streams, a record for the platform. Earnings call

One caveat on the comparison: the UFC ran 12 events in the quarter, three numbered cards and nine Fight Nights, against 10 a year earlier. Two extra shows flatter every year-over-year number.

Across the whole company, TKO's revenue rose 21% to $1.36 billion, adjusted EBITDA rose 54% to $555.7 million and net income was $277.9 million. TKO now expects $5.79 billion to $5.83 billion in revenue for 2026 and $2.295 billion to $2.315 billion in adjusted EBITDA. It also said it will use new debt and cash on hand to finish its $3 billion stock buyback.

The one soft line was boxing. TKO's Corporate and Other group, which holds its boxing fees and PBR, slipped to $61.3 million from $63.3 million. TKO said boxing revenue fell because 2025 included a one-time gain when its first equity stake in Zuffa Boxing vested. Executives still described Zuffa Boxing as the place where the company's "enterprise value growth" is.

Why it matters

The UFC is turning more of each dollar into profit than at any point in TKO's reporting, and it is doing it while fighters argue in federal court in Las Vegas, in Johnson v. Zuffa, that the company holds their pay down. A 55% margin is the kind of number that ends up in a brief. It also sets the bar for every boxing promoter trying to compete with Zuffa Boxing for fighters: TKO can afford to spend.

Business Intel

Photos via Wikimedia Commons. Tyson Fury: Mike DiDomizio, CC BY-SA 4.0 (2017 file photo); Anthony Joshua: JazzyJoeyD, CC BY-SA 4.0 (2019 file photo).

Netflix buys Fury vs. Joshua worldwide. Tyson Fury vs. Anthony Joshua streams on Netflix on Friday, Dec. 11, from Principality Stadium in Cardiff. On TKO's earnings call, Shapiro said IMG "sold the media rights to Netflix globally" and will sell the sponsorship inventory itself. Deontay Wilder (45-4-1, 43 KOs) vs. Jai Opetaia (31-0, 24 KOs) was added as the co-main event. ESPN

Polymarket puts its name on UFC 334. The UFC's Nov. 14 card at Madison Square Garden is officially "Polymarket UFC 334: Gane vs. Hokit," a prediction market's name on a numbered event with two title fights. The fee was not disclosed. UFC.com

Shakur Stevenson and Zuffa Boxing split. Stevenson and Zuffa Boxing agreed to end his contract in October before he fought for the promotion. Top Rank's Bob Arum said the deal paid Stevenson $15 million "no matter who he's going to fight" and "never made any sense." Zuffa has not disclosed the terms. EssentiallySports

The UFC now sells its own ads. Under the Paramount deal, TKO sells traditional commercial ad inventory for UFC broadcasts itself. Shapiro's pitch to advertisers: 46% of the UFC audience is 18 to 34. Earnings call

Canelo is a champion again, at $79.99. Canelo Alvarez took the WBC super middleweight title from Christian Mbilli by unanimous decision on Oct. 31 in Riyadh, 117-110, 116-111 and 115-112, and is now 64-3-3 (39 KOs). DAZN sold it in the U.S. as a $79.99 pay-per-view. ESPN · Yahoo Sports

Editor's Note

Islam Makhachev's methodical dismantling of Ian Machado Garry to retain the UFC welterweight title at UFC 330 provided a stark in-cage reminder of who runs the show. Just as TKO Group Holdings was finalizing a landmark media rights deal that reshapes its entire business model, its champion put on a clinic that reinforced the value of the product. The fight itself was more of a five-round suffocation than a thriller, but it delivered a decisive result at the top of the sport’s most important promotion, setting a new record for consecutive UFC wins in the process.

The weekend also saw movement in boxing's middleweight division. Claressa Shields handled her business against Kaye Scott in Atlanta, while across the Atlantic, Aaron McKenna picked up the vacant IBF middleweight title with a clear decision over Etinosa Oliha in Dublin. Good, solid results that keep the gears of the sport turning, though neither carried the divisional or promotional weight of the UFC's main event in Philadelphia. Both wins set up potential unification paths down the line, but for now they simply solidify the top of the 160-pound rankings.

But the biggest shockwave this week came from outside the ring. The UFC's reported $7.7 billion, seven-year pact with Paramount spells the end of the promotion's pay-per-view model in the United States. It's a seismic shift that guarantees revenue and broadens the audience on Paramount+ and CBS, fundamentally altering how American fans consume the sport. While the action in the cage dictates the rankings, this deal dictates the financial reality for the entire industry for the better part of the next decade.

Main Story

MAKHACHEV OUTPOINTS GARRY, RETAINS UFC 170 TITLE IN GRUELING DECISION

Islam Makhachev retained his UFC welterweight title with a dominant, if not electrifying, unanimous decision victory over challenger Ian Machado Garry in the main event of UFC 330 on Saturday night in Philadelphia. The win, Makhachev's record-breaking 15th consecutive victory inside the Octagon, was a masterful display of control and pressure that left the previously undefeated Garry looking out of his depth. All three judges scored the bout for the champion, though scorecards were not immediately made available.

The fight followed a clear pattern for 25 minutes. Garry (17-1, 8 KOs) attempted to use his length and striking from distance, but Makhachev (29-1, 5 KOs) consistently closed the distance, initiating grappling exchanges against the fence and on the mat. While Garry showed decent takedown defense and an ability to get back to his feet, he spent the vast majority of the fight defending, unable to mount any significant offense of his own. Makhachev landed the harder shots in the few extended striking exchanges and controlled nearly every minute of the contest with his relentless pressure and grappling acumen.

After the fight, Makhachev was critical of Garry’s friendly demeanor during the bout, which included talking and hugging. “This is a fight. This is not a game,” Makhachev said in his post-fight interview. The champion's performance was a tactical masterpiece, neutralizing a dangerous striker and demonstrating why he is among the best pound-for-pound fighters in the world. He simply had too many tools for the challenger.

UFC CEO Dana White was more critical of the challenger's performance, questioning his urgency. White said Garry lacked a “killer instinct” and never seemed to fight with the desperation required to unseat a dominant champion. The loss is the first of Garry's professional career and sends the brash Irishman back to the drawing board. For Makhachev, the victory further cements his legacy and sets up potential superfights, though no specific next opponent was named.

Fight Card Previews

What's on Deck

Rolando Romero vs. Teofimo Lopez Jr. (August 22)

Two of boxing's most volatile and powerful punchers meet for the WBA welterweight title in a can't-miss clash at the T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas. Romero is making the first defense of the title he controversially won against Ismael Barroso, while Lopez, a former unified lightweight champion, is looking to become a two-division titlist. The stylistic matchup promises fireworks, as both men possess one-punch knockout power and a willingness to trade. Lopez is the more skilled boxer, but Romero's awkward timing and concussive power make him a live underdog. For the promoters, it is a high-risk, high-reward fight that could produce a new star or send a former one crashing back to earth.

Mikaela Mayer vs. Chantelle Cameron (August 29)

In what is arguably one of the best fights that can be made in women's boxing, former unified junior lightweight champion Mikaela Mayer challenges former undisputed junior welterweight queen Chantelle Cameron for three world titles at 154 pounds. The bout, taking place in Birmingham, England, pits Mayer's technical boxing and volume against Cameron's relentless pressure and strength. Both fighters are coming off career-defining wins and losses and are desperate to regain championship hardware. It is a true 50-50 fight on paper and a massive event for women's boxing, with the winner positioned as one of the faces of the sport.

Umar Nurmagomedov vs. Song Yadong (August 29)

A pivotal bantamweight contender's bout headlines UFC's return to Shanghai. The undefeated Nurmagomedov, cousin of the legendary Khabib, brings his suffocating Dagestani grappling against the crisp, powerful boxing of China's own Song Yadong. It is a classic grappler versus striker matchup with massive divisional implications. A win for Nurmagomedov likely puts him in line for a title shot, while a victory for Song on home soil would be the biggest of his career and catapult him into the top five. The UFC is banking on Song's drawing power in China to make this a significant international Fight Night.

Business Intel

UFC LANDS RECORD $7.7B RIGHTS DEAL WITH PARAMOUNT, ENDS US PPV MODEL

The Ultimate Fighting Championship has agreed to a massive seven-year, $7.7 billion domestic media rights deal with Paramount, a move that will make Paramount+ the exclusive U.S. home for all UFC events beginning in 2027. The agreement, first reported by SportsBusiness Journal, marks a fundamental shift in the promotion's distribution strategy, most notably ending the decades-old pay-per-view model for American consumers.

The deal carries an average annual value of approximately $1.1 billion, nearly doubling the value of the promotion's previous contract with ESPN. Under the new terms, all 13 of the UFC's annual numbered events, previously sold as individual pay-per-views, will now stream exclusively on Paramount+. The platform will also be the home for the UFC’s 30 annual Fight Night events. A select number of major events are also expected to be simulcast on the CBS broadcast network, providing the UFC with a significant platform on network television.

For TKO Group Holdings, the UFC's parent company, the deal provides a massive and predictable revenue stream, de-risking the event business from the fluctuations of monthly PPV buyrates. It represents a major bet by Paramount on the UFC's consistent drawing power to drive and retain subscribers for its streaming service. The deal also reportedly includes rights for the nascent Zuffa Boxing promotion, giving Paramount a significant foothold across combat sports.

The move away from PPV is the single biggest structural change in the American combat sports landscape in a generation. It puts immense pressure on professional boxing, which remains heavily reliant on the pay-per-view model to finance its biggest fights. The boxing world will be watching closely to see if it can continue to justify the premium price point for fans when its largest competitor is now available for a monthly subscription fee.

Fighter Spotlight

IAN MACHADO GARRY

For 17 professional fights, Ian Machado Garry looked like the future of the welterweight division. The undefeated Irishman blended a slick, long-range striking game with a confidence that bordered on cockiness, drawing comparisons to a young Conor McGregor. He backed it up in the cage, rattling off seven straight wins in the UFC to earn a title shot against one of the most dominant champions in the sport, Islam Makhachev. On Saturday at UFC 330, that hype train was unceremoniously derailed in a lopsided, five-round decision loss.

The defeat wasn't a brutal knockout or a quick submission. It was a 25-minute lesson in elite mixed martial arts. Makhachev neutralized Garry's striking, controlled the clinch, and demonstrated the vast gap that exists between a top contender and a pound-for-pound king. While Garry (17-1) showed heart and a solid chin, he had no answers for the champion’s suffocating pressure. The loss exposed a critical vulnerability in his game, specifically his ability to fight effectively while moving backward and his defensive grappling against a world-class opponent.

Commercially, Garry remains one of the more marketable fighters in the 170-pound division. He is young, well-spoken, and has a dedicated following. His earnings trajectory is still pointing up, though the loss closes the door on the most lucrative championship-level paydays for the immediate future. The key now is how the UFC manages his comeback. He is too skilled to be thrown to the wolves, but too high-profile for a soft touch.

The logical next step is a fight against a top-10 contender who is primarily a striker, allowing Garry to get back to his strengths and rebuild his confidence. A matchup against someone like Geoff Neal or a resurgent Vicente Luque would make both competitive and commercial sense. It would be a high-action fight that would test Garry's resolve without immediately throwing him back into the deep end of the division's wrestling pool. At 28 years old, he has plenty of time to evolve, but the loss to Makhachev was a clear signal that he is not yet a finished product.

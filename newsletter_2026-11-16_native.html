The Greatest Ever Just Lost to the Best Right Now. Kayla Harrison submitted Amanda Nunes by rear-naked choke at 1:29 of Round 4 on Nov. 14 at Madison Square Garden. It was Harrison's first defense of the women's bantamweight title in the co-main event of UFC 334. Harrison is now 20-1 in MMA and 4-0 in the UFC. Nunes, the former two-division champion, falls to 23-6 in her first fight in three and a half years. The fight was first booked for January at UFC 324. Harrison pulled out 11 days before that date for neck surgery. Ten months later, she finished the fighter most people call the greatest woman in the sport's history. Nunes had her moments. She landed big shots on the feet in the fourth round. Harrison took those shots, caught a Nunes kick, and dragged the fight to the mat. She took Nunes's back in a scramble, sank in the rear-naked choke, and Nunes tapped before her body even hit the canvas. It was only the second submission loss of Nunes's career. Harrison controlled most of the first three rounds with takedowns and an armbar threat in the opener, but Nunes kept getting back to her feet, and Harrison's corner told her to win the fourth. Harrison is 36, a two-time Olympic judo gold medalist, and she won this belt by submitting Julianna Pena in the second round at UFC 316 in June 2025. Nunes is 38. She retired in June 2023 as the bantamweight and featherweight champion, entered the UFC Hall of Fame in 2025, and came back to chase a third run with the bantamweight belt. The grappling gap was the story. Nunes chose to grapple with a two-time Olympic judo champion, and she paid for it every time she did. The main event was a war of its own. Ciryl Gane retained the heavyweight title with a unanimous decision over Josh Hokit, 48-47 on all three cards. Hokit, who entered 10-0, blitzed Gane for two rounds and had the Garden crowd on its feet. Gane found his second wind, took the championship rounds, and survived the most punishment he has ever taken in the UFC. The two men absorbed a combined 388 strikes. Both went to the hospital afterward, and neither spoke at the post-fight press conference. Gane won the interim title by stopping Alex Pereira in June, then was named champion when Tom Aspinall vacated. This was his first defense. [MMA Junkie](https://mmajunkie.usatoday.com/story/sports/ufc/2026/11/15/ufc-334-results-ciryl-gane-vs-josh-hokit/92793148007/) · [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/sports/harrison-submits-nunes-retain-womens-ufc-bantamweight-belt-2026-11-15/) The rest of the card delivered too. Stephen Thompson, 43, ended his career with a spinning wheel kick that stopped Charles Radtke at 2:54 of Round 3, and took a Performance of the Night bonus for it. Terrance McKinney knocked out Jim Miller in 1:53 of the first round. Caio Borralho swept Yousri Belgaroui 30-27 on all three cards. Kevin Holland and Uros Medic fought to a majority draw. Gane vs. Hokit took Fight of the Night. [UFC.com](https://www.ufc.com/news/bonus-coverage-polymarket-ufc-334-gane-vs-hokit) Then Harrison said the thing that will drive the next month of talk. "I've got a lot of decisions to make when I get home," she told Joe Rogan. "Every time I make weight, I say 'never again.' So we'll see." She stopped short of announcing her retirement, and she said she has injuries to heal. But the question hangs over the division now. Harrison is 4-0 in the UFC. She has beaten Pena and Nunes, the two biggest names at 135 pounds. There is no obvious next opponent, and there is no obvious next star if she walks away. **Why it matters** Amanda Nunes is still the greatest women's fighter ever. Kayla Harrison is the best women's fighter alive right now. Those are two different things, and Saturday night proved it. Harrison did not outpoint a faded legend. She took the greatest of all time down, took her back, and choked her out in front of a sold-out Garden. If Harrison retires now, she leaves as the woman who beat the GOAT in her first title defense, and the bantamweight division loses its biggest star the moment it finally got one. The UFC spent three years building to this fight. It cannot afford to have the winner walk away before the division catches up. **What to watch** - Harrison's decision: retire, defend the belt, or move up to featherweight? - Nunes: does she retire for good, or take one farewell fight? - Gane's next defense: who challenges after Hokit pushed him to the edge? - UFC 335 on Dec. 12: Charles Oliveira defends the BMF title against Diego Lopes in Las Vegas.

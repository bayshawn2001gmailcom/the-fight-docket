Main Story

Schofield outpoints Bahdi for the vacant WBA lightweight title

Floyd "Kid Austin" Schofield won the vacant WBA lightweight title with a unanimous decision over Lucas Bahdi on Saturday at Wintrust Arena in Chicago. The scores were 116-110, 119-108 and 115-111, all for Schofield. He went into the fight 20-0 with 13 KOs and came out 21-0. Bahdi, an MVP fighter who was also unbeaten, dropped to 20-2 with 15 KOs. The bout was on DAZN.

Schofield got the first big moment in the third round, when he knocked Bahdi down. He then controlled much of the fight with speed, movement and sharp combinations. Bahdi was never out of it. He landed several heavy shots and scored a knockdown of his own in the 11th round. Schofield stayed composed, kept landing the cleaner punches and did enough over 12 rounds to win his first world title.

Both men were ending long layoffs, and it showed in a fight that swung more than a 119-108 card suggests. Bahdi's 11th-round knockdown is the reason he can argue for a rematch. Schofield's poise after the first knockdown is the reason he has the belt.

The co-main event was a bigger upset. Sergio "Yoreme" Mendoza took the WBA and WBC flyweight titles from Ricardo Sandoval by fifth-round TKO. Mendoza was a late replacement. He dropped Sandoval twice in the second round and again in the fifth, and the referee stopped it. Mendoza is the subject of our Fighter Spotlight below.

Business Intel

The promoter on the winning card is the one being sued

Whose card was it? Golden Boy and Most Valuable Promotions presented Saturday's Chicago card, which aired live on DAZN. MVP is the company Heather Hardy sued on September 23, and the complaint names MVP co-founder Jake Paul. The same company is the house behind a new world champion and a defendant in a negligence suit over a 2023 fight. Hardy's complaint says MVP was behind that fight. MVP says Lou DiBella arranged it.

The other business story of the past two weeks is title inventory. Denzel Bentley gave up his WBO middleweight title in late September to sign a multi-fight deal with Dana White's Zuffa Boxing. The math explains it: Yahoo Sports reported the purse bid for his mandatory defense was just $200,000. A Zuffa contract is worth more than a belt that pays that little to defend. Bentley is not the first champion to make that trade, and every one who does leaves a vacant title for a sanctioning body to re-sort.

Fighter Spotlight

Sergio "Yoreme" Mendoza

Sergio "Yoreme" Mendoza came in as a late replacement on the Chicago card and walked out with the WBA and WBC flyweight titles. He stopped Ricardo "El Nino" Sandoval by fifth-round TKO. Sandoval came in 27-3 with 18 KOs, and Mendoza, from Hermosillo, Mexico, is now 29-1 with 25 KOs.

Mendoza credited the scale. He said he was at his natural weight of 112 pounds and had a full training camp, and that was the difference. He dropped Sandoval twice in the second round, and Sandoval tried to adjust over the next three rounds. Mendoza scored a third knockdown in the fifth. Sandoval got back to his feet, but the referee determined he could not continue and stopped the fight.

"I wasn't concerned about a comeback by Sandoval. He was badly hurt after the two knockdowns, and it was just a matter of time."

Mendoza said after the fight that he loved fighting in front of so many Mexican fans in Chicago, and that it felt like fighting at home. He stepped in on short notice and left with two belts.

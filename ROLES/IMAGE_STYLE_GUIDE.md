# The Fight Docket — Visual Guide
### Image Generation for Nano Banana 2

---

## Core Philosophy

Images exist to make the reader feel the story before they read it. Every image should be a direct, creative interpretation of what that specific story is *about* — its theme, emotion, and stakes. There is no single mandatory style. The story dictates the visual.

**The two hard rules that never change:**
1. **No real faces or identifiable likenesses** (legal/ethical — always)
2. **No trademarked logos or brand marks** (UFC, PFL, Netflix, etc.)

Everything else is in service of the story.

---

## Step 1 — Read the Story, Find the Core

Before writing a prompt, identify two things:

**The Subject** — what is this story literally about?
(A lawsuit. A pay dispute. A championship fight. A media deal. A fighter's comeback.)

**The Emotion/Tension** — what does the reader *feel* reading it?
(Outrage. Anticipation. Betrayal. Triumph. Uncertainty. Power.)

The best image captures both. A legal story about fighter pay isn't just "contracts" — it's *exploitation, power imbalance, money withheld*. Let that tension drive the visual.

---

## Step 2 — Choose the Right Visual Approach

Different stories call for different styles. Pick the one that fits.

| Story Type | Visual Approach | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Legal / antitrust / lawsuit | Symbolic, atmospheric, conceptual | Scales of justice in arena light, stacks of legal briefs with a glove resting on top, empty courtroom with dramatic shadows |
| Fighter pay / contract dispute | Tension-driven, raw | Hands holding crumpled contract, cash on a gym floor, a negotiating table with two chairs and nothing else |
| Championship fight / big event | Epic, cinematic, scale | Empty arena from above at night, a single spotlight on a ring canvas, two sets of gloves suspended in mid-air |
| Saudi investment / money / power | Wealth, scale, grandeur | Gold light over a city skyline, oil and steel abstract, vast stadium under floodlights |
| Media deal / broadcasting | Modern, technological | TV cameras at ringside, broadcast light flooding a press conference, screens reflecting on glass |
| Political / sanctioning body | Gravitas, institutional | Empty boardroom, gavel on a table, flags in dim light |
| Fighter comeback / human interest | Intimate, personal, emotional | Worn gloves on a locker room bench, a fighter's hands wrapping tape, a solitary figure in a gym |
| Training / camp story | Kinetic, gritty, physical | Motion blur of bag work, chalk dust, sweat, worn leather up close |
| Retirement / legacy | Reflective, melancholy | A hung-up pair of gloves, empty ring at dusk, championship belt under dust |

These are starting points — not limits. If a different visual interpretation is more powerful for the specific story, use it.

---

## Step 3 — Build the Prompt

Structure prompts to give Nano Banana 2 full creative latitude. Include:

1. **Medium/style** — be specific and varied. Don't default to one look.
2. **Subject** — tied to the story's core theme
3. **Atmosphere/setting** — where does this feel like it exists?
4. **Lighting** — this is always important; be intentional
5. **Mood/emotion** — name the feeling
6. **Exclusions** — always include: no faces, no logos, no text

**Style options to rotate through (don't always pick the same one):**
- `Cinematic film still, 35mm grain`
- `Fine art documentary photography`
- `Abstract conceptual photography`
- `Dramatic editorial photograph`
- `Atmospheric wide-angle environmental shot`
- `Intimate close-up photojournalism`
- `Bold graphic photography with strong shadows`
- `Moody long-exposure sports photography`
- `High-contrast black and white editorial`
- `Rich color cinematic, warm tones / cool tones`

**Color range — use what fits the story:**
- Legal/money/power → deep golds, steel blues, monochrome
- Fight/action → high contrast, punchy, vivid
- Human interest/legacy → warm amber, muted, nostalgic
- Corruption/dispute → cold blues, shadows, desaturated
- Big event/spectacle → dramatic light, deep blacks with bright highlights

---

## Prompt Template

```
[Style: cinematic film still / fine art documentary / abstract conceptual / etc.],
[Subject tied directly to story theme and emotion],
[Environmental context: where does this exist?],
[Lighting: be specific — arena spotlights / single overhead lamp / golden hour / cold blue fill],
[Mood: tense / triumphant / melancholy / ominous / electric / raw],
[Color treatment: warm tones / cold steel / high contrast monochrome / rich saturated / etc.],
no faces, no logos, no text, no brand marks
```

**Example — antitrust lawsuit story:**
```
Fine art documentary photograph, scales of justice casting long shadows across an empty boxing ring canvas,
dramatic single spotlight from above, cold blue ambient light, 
mood of institutional power and suppressed anger,
high contrast desaturated with deep shadow,
no faces, no logos, no text
```

**Example — Fury vs. Joshua announcement:**
```
Cinematic wide-angle film still, two heavyweight championship belts laid side by side on a press conference table,
floodlights reflecting off polished surfaces, stadium blurred in background,
electric anticipatory mood, rich warm-to-cool gradient lighting,
no faces, no logos, no text
```

**Example — fighter pay investigation:**
```
Intimate close-up photojournalism, worn boxing gloves resting on a crumpled contract with dollar amounts visible as blurred abstraction,
single overhead tungsten lamp, hard shadows, 
raw and somber mood, desaturated warm tones with deep shadow,
no faces, no logos, no visible text
```

## Fighter Matchup / Standoff Images

When a section mentions two specific fighters by name facing each other (fight announcement, contract standoff, press conference, rivalry), generate a standoff or confrontation image. This creates some of the most powerful visuals in the newsletter.

**The rule:** No identifiable faces — this is a legal line, not a style preference. Generating a realistic likeness of a real person without consent is a right-of-publicity issue.

**What works powerfully instead:**
- Two fighters in silhouette facing each other under arena spotlights
- From-behind shot of two fighters at center ring or a press conference table
- Two pairs of gloves extended toward each other, nearly touching
- Shadow figures in a staredown — hard light casting long opposing shadows
- Two spotlights converging on an empty canvas
- Abstract "two forces" — opposing weight, stance, scale

**Use fighter-specific context in the prompt even without faces:**
- Weight class difference → silhouette proportions reflect it (heavyweight vs. lightweight)
- Sport type → boxing stances and gloves vs. MMA guard and shorts
- Tone of the story → tense/hostile press conference energy vs. mutual respect face-off
- Setting → arena, press conference table, weigh-in stage, empty gym

**Prompt example — Canelo vs. Crawford announcement:**
```
Cinematic wide-angle film still, two boxer silhouettes facing each other in dramatic staredown
at a dimly lit press conference stage, powerful arena spotlights from above casting long opposing shadows,
one figure slightly taller and leaner, electric anticipatory tension,
rich deep contrast with cold blue ambient light and warm spotlight center,
no faces, no logos, no text
```

**Prompt example — heavyweight title fight:**
```
Dramatic fine art photograph, two large silhouettes squaring off at center ring,
corner lights backlighting both figures from opposite sides,
canvas stretching between them, crowd blur in deep background,
electric and ominous mood, high contrast monochrome with single warm spotlight,
no faces, no logos, no text
```

---

## The Only Fixed Rules

- **No faces** — silhouettes, backs, hands, gear, shadows, from-behind shots: yes. Identifiable faces: never. This is a legal line (right of publicity), not a style choice.
- **No logos** — no real org or brand marks in the image.
- **Tied to the story** — the image must connect to what the article is actually about. Always written AFTER the newsletter section is complete, never speculatively.
- **No AI-default aesthetics** — avoid purple skies, glowing energy rings, hyper-saturated gradients. Ground it in something real.

---

*The goal: someone should be able to glance at the image and immediately understand what kind of story they're about to read — not just "this is a combat sports newsletter."*

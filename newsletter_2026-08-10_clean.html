Editor's Note

Three world titles changed hands on Saturday night, and the two cards that produced them were run by companies that are now direct competitors. TKO's Zuffa Boxing put on Dublin. Jake Paul's Most Valuable Promotions put on Orlando, nine days after announcing its merger with the Professional Fighters League. A weekend that looks like a run of good fights is better understood as two promoters building championship inventory they own.

My read is that the interesting variable this year is no longer distribution. That question got answered when Paramount took exclusive U.S. UFC rights for seven years at roughly $1.1 billion annually and the pay-per-view model went away. What is unsettled is supply: who can put title fights on the calendar, how often, and under whose banner. The MVP-PFL merger is a bet on that question, and TKO's decision to run its own boxing promotion rather than rent talent to someone else's is the same bet from the other direction.

This issue covers the three new titleholders and the promotional context that produced them, the merger's financing and integration timeline against TKO's $1.55 billion quarter, and the career economics of Quillan Salkilld's upset in Las Vegas. A correction is also due. The July 20 and July 27 issues described TKO as being inside an exclusive three-month negotiating window with ESPN for U.S. media rights. That was wrong. The ESPN window ran in early 2025 and produced no deal; Paramount holds those rights now. The error was mine, and the Business Intel section below lays out the actual structure.

Main Story

THREE BELTS CHANGE HANDS IN ONE NIGHT AS TKO AND MVP RUN COMPETING CARDS

Three world titles changed hands on Saturday night, August 8, across two cards run by two rival promoters, and a fourth headline result landed in Las Vegas the same evening. The scheduling collision is the story. TKO's boxing arm and Jake Paul's Most Valuable Promotions put championship inventory in the market on the same night, nine days after MVP announced its merger with the Professional Fighters League.

At the 3Arena in Dublin, Aaron McKenna won the vacant IBF middleweight title by unanimous decision over Etinosa Oliha, taking the cards 118-110, 117-111 and 116-112. McKenna moved to 21-0 and handed Oliha his first defeat, becoming Ireland's first middleweight world champion since Andy Lee. The bout headlined Zuffa Boxing 10, which matters as much as the result: TKO now has a homegrown Irish champion at 160 pounds and a Dublin market that just sold out for him.

At the Caribe Royale in Orlando, MVP ran MVPW-5 and produced two more new champions. Tammara Thibeault of Shawinigan, Quebec, took the IBF, WBO and Ring Magazine middleweight titles from Australia's Desley Robinson on a shutout unanimous decision, all three judges scoring it 100-90. Thibeault did it in her fifth professional fight and fought most of it with a broken nose after an accidental head clash. She now sits one belt from undisputed. In the main event, Dina Thorslund shut out Cherneka Johnson by the same 100-90 margin across all three cards to take the WBA, WBC, IBF and WBO bantamweight titles and become undisputed champion.

In MMA, the night's biggest result was not a title change but had a similar disruptive effect. At UFC Fight Night in Las Vegas, unranked Quillan Salkilld submitted Mateusz Gamrot with a rear-naked choke at 4:25 of the first round. The result inserts Salkilld into the lightweight picture and derails Gamrot's path to a title shot. The financial delta is significant: Salkilld moves from a standard fight purse to a position where he can command main-event money and contender-level contract terms.

What this signals is that promotional capacity, not talent, is now the constraint in women's boxing and at middleweight. MVP crowned two champions on a single card because it controls the matchmaking and the broadcast slot. TKO crowned one because it now runs its own boxing promotion rather than renting talent to someone else's. Both companies are building title inventory they own outright, and the merged MVP-PFL entity will enter 2027 holding a meaningful share of it.

Legal Tracker

Active Federal Cases

Johnson et al. v. Zuffa, LLC (D. Nev. 2:21-cv-01189)
Last activity: Feb. 25, 2026. Plaintiffs moved for severe sanctions, up to and including default judgment. The motion alleges that TKO Operating Co. LLC, majority owner Endeavor Group Holdings and operator Zuffa LLC destroyed years of evidence relevant to the antitrust claims and then spent months concealing the spoliation. The motion followed a two-day spoliation hearing on Feb. 4 and 5 at which UFC chief executive Dana White was the first witness, prompted by gaps in the production of cell phone data. Whether sanctions issue turns on the court's finding as to intent to deprive. This is the most consequential pending motion against the company.
Status: Motion for sanctions pending.

Cirkunovs et al. v. Zuffa, LLC (D. Nev.)
Filed May 23, 2025. A separate class action brought on behalf of fighters who competed from July 1, 2017 onward but signed contracts containing arbitration or class-action waivers. Beyond damages, the suit asks the court to invalidate those clauses in existing UFC contracts and bar them from future ones. That remedy is the one to watch: it would remove the mechanism the company currently uses to keep a large share of its roster out of collective litigation.
Status: In discovery.

No new disciplinary actions, suspensions, or adverse drug test findings were reported by the NYSAC, CSAC, or NSAC in the past week's available records.

Fight Card Previews

UFC 330 Headlines a Loaded Stretch

UFC 330: Makhachev vs. Machado Garry
Saturday, August 15 · Xfinity Mobile Arena, Philadelphia

Islam Makhachev defends the welterweight title against Ian Machado Garry, the division's number one contender. This is the first UFC numbered event of the second half of the year to stream under the Paramount+ structure with no pay-per-view charge attached, which changes what the card is actually measuring. There is no buy rate to report. The metrics that matter now are subscriber additions and retention through the weekend, and those numbers are the ones Paramount will point to when it evaluates a deal averaging $1.1 billion a year.

Watch the gate rather than the stream. Philadelphia is a first-time host for a numbered event at Xfinity Mobile Arena, and live-event revenue is the segment TKO still books directly. Early prelims open at 5pm ET, prelims at 7pm, main card at 9pm.

Also on the calendar:

  • Aug. 22 · UFC Fight Night: Hernandez vs. Rodrigues, Golden 1 Center, Sacramento
  • Aug. 29 · UFC Fight Night: Nurmagomedov vs. Song, Oriental Sports Center, Shanghai
  • Sept. 5 · UFC Fight Night: Hooker vs. Parnasse, Accor Arena, Paris

The Shanghai and Paris dates are worth watching as business events rather than sporting ones. Both slot into the international rights strategy laid out in this issue's Business Intel, feeding the same market expansion that produced the Paramount+ Canada and DAZN Germany deals.

Romero vs. Lopez
Saturday, August 22 · T-Mobile Arena, Las Vegas

Rolando Romero makes the first defense of the WBA welterweight title against Teofimo Lopez, and this one runs on the old model: a PBC pay-per-view carried on DAZN and Prime Video. Worth watching precisely because of that contrast. The same weekend the UFC gives away a Fight Night inside a subscription, boxing is still asking for an incremental payment on top of one. Romero is 17-2 and has not fought since taking the vacant belt from Ryan Garcia in Times Square last May; he was elevated to full champion in August after Jaron Ennis vacated. Lopez is 22-2 and chasing a title in a third weight class.

Business Intel

PFL MERGES INTO MVP AS TKO POSTS $1.55B QUARTER AND RAISES GUIDANCE

The Professional Fighters League is merging with Jake Paul's Most Valuable Promotions, a deal announced July 30 that creates a single boxing and MMA platform positioned against TKO Group Holdings. Jake Paul and Nakisa Bidarian will lead the combined company, with PFL chief executive John Martin staying on as CEO of MVP MMA and taking a board seat. The financing comes principally from UAE-based 885 Capital and from Knighthead Capital Management, both of which increased their PFL stakes at the start of this year. The PFL brand goes away: reporting indicates a brand migration over the coming months, with full integration under the MVP name by the start of 2027.

The competitive question is whether a merged MVP can put championship-grade inventory on the calendar at a rate that matters. Saturday's MVPW-5 card in Orlando is the early evidence, and it produced two new champions including a new undisputed bantamweight titleholder. That is the asset the merger is actually accumulating.

On the other side, TKO's domestic distribution question is settled, which is worth restating because it changes how every card this year should be read. Paramount holds exclusive U.S. UFC rights under a seven-year agreement worth roughly $7.7 billion, an average of $1.1 billion annually, running from 2026. The traditional pay-per-view model is gone. UFC's full slate of numbered events and Fight Nights streams on Paramount+, with select numbered events simulcast on CBS. The international build continues on top of that: Paramount+ becomes the exclusive home of UFC numbered event main cards in Canada for six years starting in 2027, DAZN renewed for Germany and Austria, and Arena Sport picked up the Balkans across Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia and Slovenia.

The financials support the strategy. TKO reported Q2 2026 revenue of $1.547 billion, up 18 percent year over year, with net income of $303.9 million, up 11 percent, and adjusted EBITDA of $649.9 million, up 23 percent. The company raised full-year guidance on both revenue and adjusted EBITDA. It also disclosed an approximate $30 million loss on the UFC Freedom 250 event at the White House in June, while pointing to earned media and the Washington relationships the event generated. That is a company comfortable treating a nine-figure event as a marketing line item.

Fighter Spotlight

QUILLAN SALKILLD

Quillan Salkilld's first-round submission of Mateusz Gamrot at UFC Vegas 120 was not just an upset; it was a fundamental alteration of his career's economic and competitive trajectory. The Australian took the fight to Gamrot where Gamrot was supposed to be strongest, working calmly out of a threatened heel hook before taking the back and finishing with a rear-naked choke at 4:25 of the first round. It was his first UFC main event and his fourth consecutive first-round finish. Gamrot, widely considered to be on the cusp of a title shot, saw that momentum erased in under five minutes.

The financial implications of this single victory are profound. Salkilld's market value has increased by an order of magnitude. His next contract negotiation will start from a position of immense leverage, allowing his management to command figures typical of a main-event talent. Performance bonuses, sponsorship opportunities, and a higher base pay are now on the table. He has effectively leapfrogged years of divisional climbing, bypassing the arduous and less lucrative path of grinding out wins on the undercard.

From a competitive standpoint, the matchmaking office now has a marketable Australian to move up the lightweight card, and it has him at a useful moment, with UFC 330 deciding the welterweight belt and the lightweight queue unsettled. The logical next booking is a ranked gatekeeper rather than an immediate title eliminator, which protects the asset while testing whether the finishing rate holds against elite defensive grappling. Commercially it sells itself: the underdog who produced one of the year's biggest upsets takes his next test. For Salkilld, the pressure is now to prove that Saturday night was not an anomaly, but the new standard.
